FAQ's

What organs and tissues can be donated?

Organs that can be donated include: heart, kidneys, liver, lungs, pancreas, and intestines. Tissues that can be donated include: corneas, skin, bone, heart valves, blood vessels, and connective tissue. One donor can save up to 8 lives through organ donation and enhance the lives of 75+ people through tissue donation.

How does blockchain technology improve organ donation?

Blockchain technology provides secure, transparent, and tamper-proof records of organ donations. It ensures data integrity, prevents fraud, enables real-time tracking of organ matches, and creates an immutable audit trail. This technology helps build trust between donors, recipients, and healthcare providers while streamlining the matching process.

What is the difference between living and deceased donation?

Living donation occurs when a living person donates an organ (like a kidney or part of the liver) to someone in need. Deceased donation happens after a person has been declared brain dead and their organs are donated. Living donation can be directed (to a specific person) or non-directed (to anyone on the waiting list). Both types save lives and are equally valuable.

How do I register as an organ donor?

You can register as an organ donor through our platform by clicking "Pledge To Become A Donor" and filling out the registration form. You can also register through your state's donor registry, at your local DMV, or through the National Donate Life Registry. It's important to discuss your decision with your family so they understand your wishes.

What are the medical criteria for organ donation?

Medical criteria vary by organ and are determined at the time of death. Factors include age, medical history, cause of death, and current health status. Most people can donate some organs and tissues regardless of age or medical history. The transplant team evaluates each potential donor individually to determine what can be safely donated.

How is organ matching determined?

Organ matching is based on several factors including blood type, body size, medical urgency, geographic distance, and time on the waiting list. Our blockchain platform uses advanced algorithms to ensure fair and efficient matching while maintaining transparency. The system prioritizes patients based on medical need and compatibility.

Can I change my mind about being a donor?

Yes, you can change your donor status at any time. You can update your registration through our platform or contact your state's donor registry. It's important to inform your family of any changes to your wishes. Your decision to donate is always voluntary and can be modified as your circumstances change.

What happens to my body after donation?

Organ donation is performed with the same care and respect as any other surgery. The donor's body is treated with dignity throughout the process. Donation does not disfigure the body or interfere with traditional funeral arrangements, including open-casket services. The donor's family can still have a normal funeral and burial or cremation.
